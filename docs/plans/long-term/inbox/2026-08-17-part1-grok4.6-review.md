# Review of Part 1 — Proposed Revisions

Part 1 is unusually strong: it separates **business families** from **evolution dimensions**, refuses a single shell, and already encodes the hard invariants (operation parity, publisher/subscriber ownership, candidate ≠ evaluator ≠ promoter). The gaps are not “add more SaaS features.” They are places where the option space is **underspecified enough to cause a worse Part 2**, or where a missing capability will make the platform **less reliable, less performant, or less commercially real**.

Below: each change with rationale, then a unified git-diff against the original.

---

## Change 1 — Add a fourth *constraint family*: Time, Cost, and Failure as first-class product surfaces

**Problem.** Cost, latency, and failure appear as table cells (budgets, metering, “cost and failures”) but never as a **use-mode**. Every family will die on unbounded agent loops, silent partial failure, or “it took 14 minutes and $4.” Without a first-class **bounded execution & degradation** story, Part 2 will invent ad-hoc timeouts.

**Why better.** Makes the product *usable* (users see spend and can stop work), *reliable* (partial results, retries, idempotency are product, not infra), *performant* (budgets force routing and caching), *commercially honest* (margin tracking starts in the use case, not in billing).

**What to add.** A short §3.4 or a new §4.5 “Bounded execution” under evolution, plus inventory rows for: spend visibility, cancellation, partial artifacts, SLA/degradation modes, cache/reuse of tool results.

---

## Change 2 — Explicit **control-plane vs data-plane vs model-plane** (without jumping to implementation)

**Problem.** Sovereignty (§5.6, §6.14) lists residency and replaceable providers but not the *business* split customers actually buy: who holds **identity & policy**, who holds **bytes**, who runs **models**. Swiss/EU/local mixes are the default, not the exception.

**Why better.** Preserves architecture: Part 2 can keep Agent identity portable while storage and inference move. Enables “EU data + US model with redaction” as a *capability*, not a later rewrite. More compelling for regulated buyers (clinic, fund, industrial).

---

## Change 3 — **Continuity of identity across devices, sessions, and interruption** as its own requirement (not only “durable work”)

**Problem.** §6.6 is about work context. It does not say: same human on phone + desktop; Agent reconnect after crash; “don’t double-send the email”; offline queue; notification that work finished.

**Why better.** Reliability (exactly-once *effects* vs at-least-once *attempts*), performance (don’t redo research), usefulness (ambient Agent is worthless if users cannot leave).

---

## Change 4 — **Deterministic core vs stochastic Agent** as a business invariant

**Problem.** Family C and formulation/portfolio examples assume calculations, constraints, and simulations. The plan never says the platform must **prefer and expose deterministic operations** (pricing, nutrition, risk math, vintage-aware series) and treat the Agent as a *proposer*, not the calculator of record.

**Why better.** Trust, audit, performance (don’t LLM a SUM), recursive improvement (evaluators need stable scores). This is the difference between a research terminal and a chat toy.

---

## Change 5 — **Effect classes and external-world irreversibility**

**Problem.** Governance says “don’t silently repeat an uncertain external effect” but there is no inventory of **effect classes**: read, write-internal, write-external, spend-money, communicate, mutate-policy. Without this, approval UX and idempotency cannot be designed once.

**Why better.** One approval model for email, wire, formulation change, and package promotion. Safer multi-agent (delegation can only narrow *effect class*). More compelling: users understand *what kind of thing* is about to happen.

---

## Change 6 — **Freshness, vintages, and cache coherence** as a capability, not a macro-only footnote

**Problem.** Data vintages appear in macro examples. Every family has the same failure: Agent reasons on stale CRM, yesterday’s prices, revoked mail token.

**Why better.** Reliability of decisions; performance via explicit cache; C-family scientific honesty; B-family “expert Agent” doesn’t hallucinate last quarter’s playbook against this morning’s pipeline.

---

## Change 7 — **Search, memory, and retrieval policy** as a named capability group

**Problem.** Knowledge sources exist; “remember preferences” exists. There is no product-level **what the Agent is allowed to remember**, **how long**, **who can see memory**, **hybrid search vs semantic model vs thread**. This is how products leak subscriber data into publisher packages.

**Why better.** Directly implements the ownership boundary in 3.2. Performance (don’t stuff entire CRM into context). Compelling (personalization without creepy cross-tenant memory).

---

## Change 8 — **Human-in-the-loop *quality of attention*** (queues, SLAs, batch approve, explain-diff)

**Problem.** “Attention and approval” is a row. Family A/B will drown users in cards. Family C will need *comparative* approval (A vs B vs incumbent).

**Why better.** Usefulness (throughput), safety (fatigue), performance (batch), compelling UX vs generic copilot.

---

## Change 9 — **Evaluation as a product object**, not only a recursive-improvement step

**Problem.** §6.10 lists evaluation but not: fixtures, holdout, leakage prevention, human rubric vs metric, cost-adjusted score, statistical humility (n too small → no auto-promote).

**Why better.** Prevents the plan’s own failure mode: “persuasive self-evaluation.” Makes Level 5 real. Platform moat.

---

## Change 10 — **Simulation / dry-run / shadow mode** before real-world test

**Problem.** The C loop jumps from Decision → Real-world test. Missing: dry-run operations, shadow Agent in production (log-only), paper trading, formulation *in silico*.

**Why better.** Reliability and cost; required for regulated and industrial; lets self-improvement activate “limited” without harming customers.

---

## Change 11 — **Composition economics and blast-radius** (multi-app)

**Problem.** §5.3/§6.13 compose nav and search. Missing: **shared spend wallet vs per-package meter**, **whose Agent runs when two apps claim the same record**, **conflict when two packages define “Account”**.

**Why better.** Without this, multi-app is a demo that destroys entitlement and semantics. Architecture-preserving: namespaces + *binding contracts*, not a mega-schema.

---

## Change 12 — **Onboarding, empty-state, and time-to-first-valuable-artifact**

**Problem.** Success conditions mention repeatability and onboarding “with less custom engineering” but there is no capability: sample data, connector wizard, first-run Agent tour, publisher-supplied starter work.

**Why better.** Commercial conversion; Family B lives or dies on first 10 minutes; doesn’t change the thesis, just makes it sellable.

---

## Change 13 — **Accessibility, i18n, and EU product reality**

**Problem.** “Sovereign European cloud” with no mention of languages, accessibility, or explainability-for-humans (not just audit logs). Weak for EU public sector and clinics.

**Why better.** Market access; trust; not a shell decision—an experience capability.

---

## Change 14 — **Threat model / abuse / prompt injection as business capabilities**

**Problem.** Safety is authorization and self-grant. Missing: **untrusted content** (email, web, subscriber files) instructing the Agent; exfiltration via tools; confused deputy across publisher tools.

**Why better.** Family B is an attack surface (publisher tools + subscriber data). Reliability of the ownership boundary. Should be Essential for B and C.

---

## Change 15 — **Clarify that chat transcripts are not the system of record**

**Problem.** Implied by Artifacts and durable work, never stated. Teams will store truth in threads.

**Why better.** Forces Part 2 toward Work + Artifact + Operation log. Better performance (don’t reparse chat). Better audit.

---

## Change 16 — **Open questions: add the ones that actually fork architecture**

Add: dual-control for spend; who is legal controller of subscriber data in published SaaS; whether packages may ship *code* vs only declarative experience in v1 of the *capability space* (already #18, sharpen); whether the platform is allowed to be a **model reseller** vs BYOK only (margin + sovereignty).

---

## Change 17 — **Capability matrix: promote a few ratings and add missing rows**

- Agent budget limits: Essential for A (not only C)—otherwise SaaS margin dies.
- Cancellation / reconnect: Essential all.
- Dry-run: Common A, Essential C.
- Untrusted-content isolation: Essential B/C.
- Outcome-unknown: already good; add **stale-data refusal**.
- Mobile: keep Common, but **approval-on-mobile** Essential for A/B (otherwise ambient Agent is desktop-only).

## What I would *not* change

- Three families + self-improvement as a mode (not a fourth market) is the right compression.
- Leaving first paid product open is correct for Part 1.
- Refusing one universal shell is the most important UX decision in the doc.
- “Smallest set of identities” instruction to Part 2 is right; my last line is only a *hint* so Part 2 does not invent 28 objects for 28 bullets.

## Highest-leverage trio if you only accept three edits

1. **Invariants §2.1** (especially deterministic core, chat ≠ record, planes, untrusted content).  
2. **Effect classes + bounded jobs** (approval, idempotency, margin, cancel).  
3. **Evaluation honesty + shadow/dry-run** (makes Level 5 and Family C real instead of rhetorical).
