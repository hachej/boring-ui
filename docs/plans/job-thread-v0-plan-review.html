Gate 1 · Plan approval

Job Thread v0 — 1 Thread = 1 job

OWNER DECISION

A projection, not a new Thread: several per-agent Pi sessions merged into one job timeline via a stateless relay, with typed handoffs, centrally-enforced caps, and an honest failure/restart contract. K7 demo — "grow audience 1,200 → 5,000" — proves it with two fleet agents (worker/reviewer) and one Objective.

Round 2 (folds fresh-eyes + cross-model review) · PR #1403 · Issue #1399 · Branch weekend/jobthread-v0-plan · No implementation before owner sign-off

What this plan builds

Twelve artifacts, nothing else. Named in product words, with the code noun underneath. Ordering lives in the Slices section — this is composition, not sequence.

All paths are under plugins/job-threads/.

One handoff turn — and what the participants share

Two boundaries, only one restrictive. Above the line, the conversation boundary is closed: only the two settled final posts cross between participants — private reasoning, tool calls and credentials never do. Below the line, the artifact boundary is open: both participants share one workspace and one canonical filesystem (D25 :410, D28 :462-463), so the reviewer reads the very files the worker wrote — nothing is copied or synced. Participants share the work, not each other’s minds and keys.

Steps 2 and 10 are the crash-safety keystone: a turn is reserved before anything is sent, so a crash can never leave an unrecorded send or an escaped cap count. The plan carries three further diagrams (data model, recovery order, edge lifecycle) as Mermaid in docs/plans/job-thread-v0-plan.md.

OptionsUnblocks / costs

4The two boundaries — what is shared, what is not?

The plan has two boundaries and only one of them is restrictive. They are easy to conflate, and conflating them makes the design read as agents walled off from each other, which is the opposite of what it says.

OptionsUnblocks / costs
Artifact boundary — OPEN. Participants on a job share one workspace and one canonical filesystem. Ratified, not invented: agents in a workspace “intentionally share filesystem/process/runtime authority while retaining distinct … session … and provenance identity” (D25 :410), and same-workspace agents get execution views “without copying the authoritative filesystem” (D28 :463). Reach is governed by mounts and per-participant tool authority, not by partition. Confirms: the reviewer reads the very files the worker wrote — nothing copied, synced, or attached. This is the half of the shared-VM feel people actually like, delivered natively and governed. Rejecting it would mean per-participant copies and a second authoritative file tree, which D28 :462 exists to prevent.
Conversation boundary — CLOSED. Only the settled final assistant message (message-end.final, gated on non-willRetry) and relay-authored system handoff markers enter another participant's prompt. No tool calls, no intermediate messages, no private reasoning, no credentials. Confirms: each participant's private trace stays reachable only by drill-down — the demo's privacy and noise story. A wider boundary would need re-litigating the handoff tool and the merged-timeline renderer.
Recommended: confirm the pairing. Participants share the work, not each other's minds and keys. Sharing context windows is what makes multi-agent setups leak secrets and drown in tool spew; sharing a filesystem is what lets them collaborate at all. v0 takes the second and refuses the first.
