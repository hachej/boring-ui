## 1. The three arcs since 2026-08-08

The eighteen days between snapshots contain three distinct arcs. They are
sequenced here in the order they happened, because the third one re-derives the
first two.

**Arc two — the multi-agent design sprint (2026-08-24 → 08-26).** The owner ran

reachable from components that already ship. In parallel, the rulings ledger
[#1399](https://github.com/hachej/boring-ui/issues/1399) accumulated ten owner

thread noun, the seat/workspace sharing boundary, and archived threads.
[#1401](https://github.com/hachej/boring-ui/pull/1401) — *"A Thread may span
multiple Seats, projected as one timeline; one Thread per job"* — **merged to
`main` on 2026-08-26**.

**Arc three — the premises correction (2026-08-26).** The owner then read his
own design sprint against the frozen kernel and re-derived kernel-first
ordering. Three of the plan's open questions turned out to be one question —
*what does a thread durably persist, and can we trust what it says about who did
what?* The answer ruled against the display-grade shortcut. The multi-agent plan
pack was re-cut the same day from "plan the UI, then build it" into a
**premises-first program** (`docs/plans/multiagent-shell/premises.md`), and
[#1416](https://github.com/hachej/boring-ui/pull/1416) was opened to ratify the
ordering into the long-term plan.

The honest reading of arc three: **it is a correction, not a discovery.** The
kernel-first ordering it lands was already frozen — Decision 29 named durable
replay's own re-evaluation trigger (`DECISIONS.md:472`/`:476`), and
`seatId`-in-C7 was already ratified as required
(`docs/plans/long-term/ratified/RECONCILIATION.md:153`). The design sprint's
value was making the cost of ignoring them concrete.

## 3. The premise program — the new critical path

Source: `docs/plans/multiagent-shell/premises.md` (chapter 1 of the canonical
pack, [#1409](https://github.com/hachej/boring-ui/pull/1409)). The ordering it
ratifies is what [#1416](https://github.com/hachej/boring-ui/pull/1416) puts
into the long-term plan.

The program's premise: a small number of kernel capabilities must land and be
**proven** before the surface that depends on them is built. Two rulings set the
tone — **the engine does not ship on conformance Level B**, and **the thread
storage model is not decided**.

| Premise | State | Unblocks | Honest sizing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[durable-streams]** (bead `wt-391-forward-9p50`, formerly P1) — Level D conformance, flag default-on, Decision 29 addendum | Scoped and beaded; the only premise genuinely ready | The whole engine | ~2 sessions, splittable into implement / flip-and-ratify |
| **[thread-storage-spike]** (formerly P2) — competitor study + in-stack technical spike | Newly scoped; nobody has run it | The engine's data-model slice, the thread noun's durable shape, #1355's conversation reference types | 1 research session + 1 spike session, partly parallel |
| **[seat-audit-attribution]** (formerly P3, seat storage / C7) — `seatId` as real envelope identity | Ratified in principle, unscoped in practice | Honest who-said-what everywhere; per-seat metering later | ~2 sessions, and genuinely uncertain — it touches the envelope |
| **[saved-views-kernel]** (formerly P4) — the first ratified View slice, contract as a set | **No sizing at all**; needs its own planning pass | Saved views in the view library | Unknown |
| **[merge-queue]** (formerly P5) | Standing obligation, not a bead | Branch health for everything | Ongoing — see §6 |
| **[gate-re-ruling]** (formerly P6) | Cannot happen early | Both owner gates | Minutes of owner time, after [thread-storage-spike] reports |

**[durable-streams] is the keystone.** The dependency analysis recorded on
#1409 scores it 10 — the single highest-leverage node in the graph. It is also
the cheapest premise that is actually ready. Everything the engine does waits on
it, and it reframes a design choice rather than merely unblocking one: the
shared-durable-log ("blackboard") shape studied against Buzz in #1399 only
becomes *possible* on a Level D substrate, so the relay-vs-blackboard decision
is deliberately deferred until the substrate is real.

**What this ordering descopes.** The engine's interim receipt machinery — a
durable receipt chain built to make relay hops idempotent on a *non-durable*
substrate — is marked `descoped-pending-P1` rather than deleted. On a Level D
substrate much of it is redundant; how much survives is a post-[durable-streams]
design question.

**The program's real risk, stated by the program itself.** [thread-storage-spike]
and [seat-audit-attribution] are **coupled** — attribution shape depends on
storage shape — and could deadlock if run as two independent spikes that each
assume the other's answer. The storage spike's decision criteria are written
specifically to prevent that: it must report how attribution lands under each
candidate.

**Substrate-free exceptions** may run in parallel with the premises, because
they are substrate-free *by construction* — a property that can be checked, not
an exemption granted: **[shell-layout]** (formerly L1, layout traits and
internal shell composition), **[shell-location]** (formerly L1.5, the
serializable location contract), and **[shell-navigation]** (formerly L2a, nav
chrome with counts **absent** as a valid state). Everything else in the shell
either renders a thread, renders evidence under an attention item, or adopts the
shell into a host — and therefore waits.

## 8. The commercial split — premises, never pricing

The 2026-08-08 ratification stands unchanged and now has a second worked
example.

**The rule.** boring-ui provides the **premises**, never the pricing. The
platform ships the capability substrate — per-user and per-workspace provider
credentials, provenance-labeled provider rows, and a per-workspace usage-facts
feed when a consumer pulls it. It stays commercially neutral and must not
preclude any pricing topology. Each app/tenant repo adapts the strategy for its
segment; billing systems live app-side.

**The worked examples.**

1. *Metering* (2026-08-08): #819 ships only when a usage-priced offer pulls it.
   Still true; still unmerged; still correctly not a blocker.
2. *Landing pages* (2026-08-10): the same rule applied to presentation. The
   owner closed #1154 on the ruling that per-agent landing pages are an **app**
   concern — the Seneca tenant app (`hachej/boring-ui-constellation`, which
   deploys `constellation.senecaapp.ai`) renders them with its own content
   module, prerender, and design system. The platform kept only the two pieces
   that are authority-adjacent: persisted per-workspace default agent (#1156)
   and the trusted signup-domain hook (#1165), both merged.

**Consequence for this refresh.** Commercial sequencing — pricing topology, the
vertical-agent go-to-market order, the outreach motions, per-segment packaging —
belongs in the Seneca tenant repo's roadmap and is deliberately **absent** from
this snapshot and from the DIRECTION revision it proposes. What this refresh
carries is the platform side of the same wave: the credential, attribution, and
usage-fact substrate those offers will need, sequenced on its own merits.

The two waves run **beside** each other. Neither gates the other, which is the
whole point of the split: a commercial decision must never be able to reorder
the kernel, and kernel sequencing must never be justified by an unnamed offer.
