# 02 — Interview the work, not only the idea

## Source-backed principle

GOV.UK recommends a discussion guide, neutral prompts, real examples, and clarification of the participant's meaning [S02](sources.md#s02). Contextual research adds observation of the real setting, tools, and artifacts [S03](sources.md#s03). Product Talk emphasizes recruiting relevant participants and collecting specific accounts of past behavior [S05](sources.md#s05).

## Boring PM application

Prepare around an uncertainty, not a list of features. Before the conversation, know which decision the interview should inform, why this participant can help, and what artifact might make their explanation concrete. Explain the purpose and how notes or recordings will be used. If recording is wanted, establish permission before recording.

Use the [technical-comfort opening and profile](user-profile.md) before exploring solutions when the relevant context is unknown. Then recover a real case. Adapt the explanation to the person while keeping domain questions deep; technical confidence and domain expertise are separate. Do not make them repeat an answer or finish a profile form before the interview can help them.

Walk through one recent case:

1. **Set the scene:** what triggered the task, who was involved, and what was at stake?
2. **Reconstruct actions:** what input arrived, what happened next, and which system or person was involved?
3. **Zoom in:** where did effort, delay, uncertainty, or a consequential decision occur?
4. **Inspect the result:** what was produced, who used it, and how was correctness determined?
5. **Compare:** ask about a second case that was difficult or different.

The sequencing is an interview aid. Follow a useful thread, then return to the missing part of the case rather than abruptly changing topics.

## Ask for evidence without making the interview burdensome

An example spreadsheet, redacted report, screen walkthrough, or task log can reveal terms and handoffs that are hard to describe. Ask for the smallest safe example that answers the question. Note whether you directly observed the task or merely saw a reconstruction. A polished demonstration may differ from ordinary work.

Capture statements individually with source locations. Do not mix the person's words with the agent's interpretation. If the expert says a step takes an hour, record a reported estimate; if an observed sample is timed, record the sample and timing conditions separately.

## Bias controls

- Delay describing your preferred solution until the current task is understood.
- Replace predictions of future usage with evidence about present behavior and constraints.
- Seek a case where the proposed explanation fails.
- Include perspectives unlike the expert's when the intended audience is broader.
- Play back an interpretation for correction without asking the participant to endorse a whole product.

## Exit and failure patterns

You should be able to retell the case from trigger to result and point to its uncertain parts. Stop adding questions when the next useful action is observation or a prototype. Avoid treating politeness, excitement, or an expert's authority as independent support for demand. Use [the evidence template](../assets/templates/02-evidence.md), then [compare candidate solutions](solution-exploration.md) rather than converting the first suggestion directly into requirements.
